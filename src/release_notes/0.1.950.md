Bookmarks land in the editor, the way VS Code's Bookmarks extension and nvim's
marks work: park a flag on any line you want to come back to, then walk the
flags without hunting for the line again.

`Cmd+Opt+Shift+K` toggles a bookmark on the cursor's line and a blue `⚑`
appears in the glyph margin, the same column the breakpoint dot uses. Debugger
state still wins that cell — a breakpoint, the stop arrow and the AI-stream
square all paint over a bookmark rather than being hidden by one — and the
test play bead now yields to it.

`Cmd+Opt+.` jumps to the next bookmark below the cursor and `Cmd+Opt+,` to the
previous one, both wrapping at the ends of the file, so a file's marks form a
ring you can spin through with one hand. The status bar names where you landed
(`Bookmark 2 of 3 (line 42)`) and says so plainly when a file has none,
instead of a key that looks broken. A bookmark inside a folded region unfolds
it on the way in and the line is centred, not scraped against the viewport
edge.

Marks follow their lines as you edit: a newline, a paste or a deleted block
above a mark moves it with its text, undo and redo move it back, a mark on a
deleted line goes with it (or settles on the line a join leaves behind), and
an external reload never leaves one past the end of the file.
`Cmd+Opt+Shift+B` clears every mark in the file.

Marks are kept per file, so switching tabs and coming back finds them where
you left them, and the palette carries all four commands (`Bookmarks: Toggle
Bookmark`, `Next`, `Previous`, and `Clear All in File`) for anyone who would
rather type than reach for a chord.
